Running is for everyone.

Why I run

Though I played tennis growing up, I was never particularly athletic. I do think team sports are incredibly fun, but the one thing I've always struggled with is how dependent they are on other people. Getting everyone together, coordinating schedules, finding the time: there's always something that gets in the way. Running, on the other hand, is wonderfully uncomplicated. You don't need anyone else. You just put on your shoes and head out.

My first few runs were absolutely miserable. I couldn't understand why anyone would voluntarily put themselves through that. But somewhere along the way, I got hooked. Not necessarily on running itself, but on the feeling of doing something I genuinely didn't think I was capable of. Setting a goal, showing up, pushing through, and slowly realising I could do a little more than I thought.

I still wouldn't call running fun. In fact, I spend a fair amount of time questioning my life choices while I'm actually out there. But the person I am after a run is almost always happier than the person who started it.

Also, I'm extremely proud of having successfully gaslit a bunch of my friends into believing running is fun, getting them to run with me, and even signing up for races together. So I guess that's another win.

So far I've run three races: a 10K, and the Delhi Half Marathon twice. My next goal is my first full marathon in 2027.
